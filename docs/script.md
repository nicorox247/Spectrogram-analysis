# Script

This document holds the full video voiceover script. It will fill in as the project develops. The structure mirrors the seven-section outline.

## Conventions

- Each section is timed to a target duration. Voice ~150 words/minute, so a 4-minute section is roughly 600 words.
- `[VISUAL: ...]` notes describe what's on screen.
- `[AUDIO: ...]` notes describe music examples (keep total under 2 minutes per rubric).
- `[CITE: ...]` notes mark where to credit a source on screen.

## Section 2: What a Spectrogram Is (2 minutes, ~300 words)

[TO DRAFT]

Key points to cover:
- Waveform shows amplitude over time but doesn't tell you what frequencies are present
- Fourier transform decomposes a signal into its constituent frequencies
- Spectrogram applies the FFT in overlapping windows so you can watch frequency content evolve
- Vertical axis = frequency (log scale), horizontal axis = time, color = intensity
- This project lives in the bottom region — sub-bass, below 60 Hz

## Section 3: The Acoustic Floor (4 minutes, ~600 words)

[TO DRAFT]

Key points:
- Bach organ piece — show low pedal notes, harmonics
- Note that the organ is the EXCEPTION; it can reach 16-32 Hz with 16-foot stops, but only because of enormous physical pipes
- Beethoven contrabasses — show fundamentals around 40-50 Hz
- The general acoustic floor for orchestral music is ~40 Hz
- Below this is essentially inaccessible to acoustic instruments
- Cite Sethares on harmonic series

## Section 4: Rock Foregrounds the Low End (4 minutes, ~600 words)

[TO DRAFT]

Key points:
- Zeppelin "When the Levee Breaks" — kick drum and bass guitar foregrounded
- Spectrogram shows energy down to ~50 Hz, almost nothing below 40
- Compare to early 60s rock recording where bass is mixed quietly
- The change is amplification, multitrack recording, stadium PA — not new spectral territory
- Cite Sterne on medium and reproduction

## Section 5: Dub and the Discovery of Sub-Bass (4 minutes, ~600 words)

[TO DRAFT]

Key points:
- Kingston, early 1970s, sound system culture
- Sound systems engineered for outdoor playback at high volume with custom bass cabinets
- Producers like King Tubby treat bass as content, not support
- Spectrogram shows energy below 40 Hz, deliberately exposed by stripping midrange
- Brief gesture forward to techno/jungle/dubstep
- Cite Veal and Henriques

---

## Section 6: The 808 and the Spectral Identity of Hip-Hop (5 minutes, ~750 words)

[TO DRAFT]

Key points:
- Roland TR-808, 1980, originally a commercial failure
- 808 kick drum is essentially a tunable sine wave with low fundamental and slow decay
- Becomes foundational to hip-hop production by the mid-80s
- Future tracks show bimodal spectral signature: heavy 808 + vocal range, gap in middle
- This shape is genuinely new in music history
- Direct lineage from dub via the drum machine
- Cite Schloss on hip-hop production

## Section 7: Conclusion (2 minutes, ~300 words)

[TO DRAFT]

Key points:
- Synthesize, don't restate
- The audible floor of music has moved down by approximately an octave in 50 years
- This is technologically enabled, not merely stylistic
- Three different aesthetic interpretations of the same opening territory
- What counts as "musical" is contingent on the technology that produces and reproduces sound
- The harmonic series is physics, but the aesthetic possibilities of the spectrum are historically constructed
- End on the side-by-side spectrogram comparison

## Drafting Notes

- Read each section aloud as you draft. Lines that look fine on the page often sound awkward.
- Aim for a conversational register — not stiff academic prose, but specific and rigorous.
- When you cite a source, do it briefly and integrate the idea: "As Jonathan Sterne argues, sound reproduction technology doesn't just transmit music, it reshapes what music is" — not "According to Sterne (2003), [paraphrased thesis]."
- Read the script through once with a stopwatch before recording. If you're over 20 minutes, cut. If you're under 18, expand the strongest section rather than padding throughout.
